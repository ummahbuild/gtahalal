# Dunya — Real World First: product and game safety policy
Status: proposed policy and implementable UI contract. No medical diagnosis, religious verdict, or gameplay release is claimed.

## Design principle
Dunya should help people **leave the screen to learn, worship, help neighbors, study, organize and build**. Completing real-life activities is optional and private. No self-reported real-world action grants a competitive currency, piety score, salvation status, leaderboard placement, financial prize or pressure to disclose faith.

## Anti-compulsion requirements
- No daily streak loss, loot boxes, endless autoplay, variable-ratio reward loops, FOMO countdowns, manipulative notifications, guilt copy or forced return times.
- Session plan before play: 10/20/30/45-minute options, with optional no-limit for accessibility; user can change or disable reminders. At chosen interval, pause safely and offer "Save and leave", "Take a break", "Continue for now". Never lock gameplay or shame.
- Respect user-initiated "I'm going to pray" or "I'm doing this in real life": immediate safe pause, local save, screen dim, no countdown, no tracking of completion or location.
- End of mission offers a finite, optional action card: talk to a real neighbor, volunteer, read with a qualified teacher, research an accessibility barrier, join a community planning meeting. Clearly distinguish **inspiration** from verified local service availability.
- No infinite-scroll feed inside the game. Default short mission arcs, natural stopping points, accessible save-and-exit and quiet notifications.
- Do not ask for prayer times, religious observance, conversion status, physical health or minors' personal data as analytics events.
- Privacy-first local settings; consent for optional cloud sync; clear deletion.
- Evaluate time-on-task and satisfaction through voluntary research, not engagement-maximization. Red flags: players missing obligations, deceptive reminders, inability to stop.
- Provide links to external support if users report harmful gaming patterns; do not diagnose.

## Research basis
WHO describes gaming disorder as impaired control, increasing priority over other activities and continuation despite harm, with significant impairment; it affects a minority of gamers. Source: https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder
FTC report documents dark patterns such as disguised ads, difficult cancellation, hidden fees and privacy manipulation. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers
These sources motivate safeguards; they do not prove Dunya is clinically protective.

## Acceptance
1. A 20-minute reminder can be acknowledged, extended or dismissed with equal prominence.
2. "I'm going to pray" pauses immediately and persists progress without a worship-completion event.
3. No timer, leaderboard, badge, reward or tracking for real prayer, shahada, dawah, wudu or volunteering.
4. A child or adult can exit at any mission stage without losing story progress.
5. Accessibility and reduced-motion users can disable reminders and read cards without time pressure.
6. No real-life activity is falsely certified by self-report.

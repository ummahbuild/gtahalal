# Dunya — Consumer Protection Research and Parody Mechanics
Research date: 2026-11-22. Public original sources; separate **verified general mechanisms** from **fictional corporation scripts**. This does not establish misconduct by any real company named in the parody bible.

## Primary-source findings
1. **FTC 2022 Dark Patterns report:** identifies disguised advertising, difficult subscription cancellation, hidden fees/terms and interfaces that steer privacy disclosure. These are documented *classes of practices*, not allegations about every company in an industry. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers
2. **FTC/ICPEN 2024 review:** among 642 subscription websites/apps examined, nearly 76% displayed at least one *possible* dark pattern; investigators did **not** determine whether the practices were unlawful. Do not generalize this sample to every website. Source: https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-icpen-gpen-announce-results-review-use-dark-patterns-affecting-subscription-services-privacy
3. **EU Digital Services Act, Articles 27 and 38:** requires transparency of recommendation parameters and, for very large platforms/search engines, at least one recommendation option not based on profiling. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065
4. **CFPB 2023 guidance:** warns that unclear recurring charges, inadequate consent and unreasonable cancellation barriers may violate US consumer financial law; jurisdiction and current legal status require review. Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-guidance-to-root-out-tactics-which-charge-people-fees-for-subscriptions-they-dont-want/
5. **WHO ICD-11:** gaming disorder involves impaired control, priority over other activities and continuation despite harms with significant impairment. It affects a minority of gamers; no basis to diagnose users based on session duration. Source: https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder

## Eleven playable consumer-protection scenarios
| Chapter | Fictional harmful mechanism | Consumer's lawful response | Institution/system to build |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention Trap / Looply | endless personalized feed, opaque ranking | switch feed controls, turn off autoplay, request explanation, advocate for transparent design | finite feed, user control, creator portability |
| Narrative Machine / Flashline | misleading headline and disguised sponsor | check primary source, seek correction, compare accounts | newsroom corrections desk and sponsor labels |
| Broken Food Chain / EverHarvest | opaque unit pricing, misleading shelf offer | compare unit prices, retain receipt, request correction and contact regulator if warranted | transparent price board and supplier records |
| Debt Empire / Credora | confusing total repayment and recurring charges | request written terms, compare total costs, preserve records, use applicable complaint process | plain-language financing disclosure |
| Watching Eye / OmniTrace | consent steering and excessive data requests | reject optional permissions, access/delete data where law permits | minimization, export, deletion, privacy review |
| Supply Chain / TitanRoute | vague provenance and delivery terms | request supplier disclosure, verify terms, use lawful dispute process | traceable contracts and procurement logs |
| Housing Trap / Skyline | hidden fees, misleading accessibility claims | obtain written cost/access terms, document conditions, seek tenant support | transparent total cost and access audits |
| Education Machine / BrightPath | confusing trial conversion and lock-in | review renewal terms, cancel through documented route, export permitted data | open curriculum and accessible exit |
| Healthcare Monopoly / CareAxis | opaque bills, privacy and referral friction | request itemized information and authorized records, seek qualified patient advocate | patient-centered service and review |
| Captured City / CivicBridge | inaccessible public consultation and procurement disclosures | request accessible hearings, inspect public records, file lawful feedback | transparent resident oversight |
| Great Distraction / Consortium | bundled dependence across services | diversify suppliers, require independent audits, use governance checks | interoperable community institutions |

## Game loop: Notice -> Verify -> Compare -> Act -> Repair
- Present a fictional interface, offer an accessible explanation of the pattern, then let the player test the choices.
- Evidence board distinguishes allegation, observation, contractual term, regulator finding and fiction.
- Show a jurisdiction selector and date on legal resources; do not claim a consumer remedy applies everywhere.
- Allow lawful, peaceful choices: use controls, document, ask provider, seek qualified help, report to relevant regulator, build alternatives.
- No defamatory naming of real companies in fictional wrongdoing scenes; all real company case studies require independently verified case outcomes and response.
- Include both real benefits and drawbacks of platforms: access, cost, convenience, creators, workers and small businesses.

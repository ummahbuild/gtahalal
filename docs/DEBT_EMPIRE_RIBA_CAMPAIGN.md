# Dunya — The Debt Empire: Break the Cycle
**Status:** original fictional campaign design, unimplemented, not a fatwa, financial recommendation or accusation about any real company. Extends Chapter 4 of MAIN_CAMPAIGN_WORLD_BIBLE.md and Credora in INDUSTRY_PARODY_CAMPAIGN_BIBLE.md. Source interpretation, Sharia compliance and jurisdictional financial rules are **pending qualified review**.

## Logline
A financing offer promises to save Bayhaven's pantry, but opaque terms threaten the people who rely on it. Ahmed or Fatimah begins with one household budget and ends by building a lawful, transparent financing ecosystem that can survive a recession without exploiting borrowers or collapsing under losses.

**Central tension:** the player cannot simply press a button to abolish interest. A neighborhood relies on existing financial institutions for wages, housing, medical care, credit and payments. The player must build practical alternatives, persuade stakeholders, navigate regulation and manage genuine financial risk without coercion.

## Parody and character ensemble
- **Credora Capital**: fictional consumer-finance conglomerate; its services are convenient and widely used, and its staff disagree over product design. Not an allegation about a specific bank or fintech.
- **Aurex Bank**: fictional commercial bank, provides payments, deposits and credit; regulators require stability.
- **HalalBridge**: fictional startup advertising itself as Islamic finance but with unresolved governance questions. Its branding is not proof of compliance.
- **The Commons Fund**: a community-owned, proposed alternative that players must responsibly capitalize and govern.
- **Mira Solis**, Credora compliance officer: believes clear disclosures and solvency matter; can become an ally.
- **Tariq Bello**, community treasurer: wants the pantry funded without exhausting donations.
- **Nadia Okafor**, small-business owner: needs equipment, cannot wait months for approvals.
- **Omar Haddad**, grocery operator: prefers predictable cash flow over ideological slogans.
- **Dr. Samira Farooq**, fictional scholar-review coordinator: flags unresolved questions rather than issuing instant rulings.
- **Jonah Reed**, consumer advocate: protects customers of all faiths and insists on informed choice.
- **Leila Hassan**, regulator: requires licensed operations, safeguarding, fair treatment and consumer recourse.
- **Rafiq Hale**, investor: offers rapid capital for restrictive control rights; neither a demon nor a secret mastermind.

## Acts, levels and exact mission loops
### Act I — The Price of Convenience (Levels 4.01–4.05)
**4.01 The Repair Bill:** Nadia's refrigeration equipment breaks, threatening pantry food. Player interviews Nadia, Omar and suppliers; maps immediate needs, grants, savings and financing options. Choices: emergency grant with limits; transparent commercial finance referral; delay purchase and arrange a temporary shared unit. Consequence: spoilage risk, runway, service access. Repair: renegotiate timeline and add backup cold storage.
**4.02 The Offer on the Screen:** Credora advertises a fictional financing product. Player compares principal, repayment schedule, total payments, fees, late charges and assumptions in a side-by-side calculator; terms are explicitly hypothetical, not actual offers. Choices: request clearer disclosures; ask independent counselor; choose a different lawful funding route. Repair: correct a misleading comparison before publication.
**4.03 The Fee Nobody Saw:** A confusing renewal charge affects several fictional residents. Player collects consent-based receipts, separates error from disputed policy and offers customer-support, lawful complaint or independent mediation routes. No bank account hacking or coercion.
**4.04 The Debt Map:** Build a household/business cash-flow model: income, essential costs, existing obligations, uncertainty and emergency reserve. Protect private financial information; no real bank connections or personal credit scores. Choices: reduce scope, improve cash flow or seek qualified assistance.
**4.05 The Hearing:** Community members, employees and regulator present competing perspectives. Player may advocate for disclosures, build an educational program or pilot a transparent alternative. A viral accusation without evidence lowers trust and requires correction.

### Act II — What Makes a Contract Fair? (Levels 4.06–4.10)
**4.06 The Scholar's Questions:** A proposed alternative is labeled Sharia-compliant. Player learns that labels are not approval: identify transaction, ownership, risk, sale/lease/service, profit mechanism, penalties and source-review status. Compare *conceptual* qard hasan, murabaha, ijara, musharaka, mudaraba and takaful models, each marked **requires scholar and legal review**; no product presented as universally permissible.
**4.07 The Asset Is Real:** Nadia needs a freezer. Player compares a conceptual cost-plus asset sale and lease to a conventional borrowing example, checks whether ownership and asset risk are actually allocated as described, and flags sham arrangements for review. No automated compliance verdict.
**4.08 Partners in Profit and Loss:** A fictional cooperative enterprise needs capital. Build a profit-sharing model, estimate downside losses, protect minority partners, disclose management fees and show why risk cannot be wished away.
**4.09 Benevolence Has a Budget:** Design a charitable revolving assistance fund with donor restrictions, fair eligibility, fraud prevention, non-humiliating collections and sustainable loss reserves. A no-fee product still costs money to operate.
**4.10 The Review Board:** Submit evidence to independent scholar, lawyer, consumer advocate and financial-risk reviewers. Each may request revision. No automatic religious approval or "halal meter"; disagreement can remain visible and the player can pause the launch.

### Act III — Build the Alternative (Levels 4.11–4.15)
**4.11 The First Hundred Members:** Design cooperative membership, voting, complaints, privacy, conflict-of-interest disclosures and financial literacy. Ensure non-Muslim customers are treated fairly and never forced into religious instruction.
**4.12 Liquidity Day:** Simulate deposits or member funds, permitted capital structures, cash needs and a withdrawal shock. Distinguish a credit cooperative from a licensed bank; do not imply deposit insurance without actual fictional regulatory status.
**4.13 Underwriting Without Humiliation:** Compare affordability and repayment capacity using fictional data, challenge biased proxy features, provide explanations and appeals, avoid surveillance of worship or personal beliefs.
**4.14 The First Default:** A borrower cannot repay after a genuine hardship. Player negotiates lawful, compassionate options, balances fairness to members and prevents an exploitative collection process. Recovery: revise underwriting and hardship reserves.
**4.15 The Audit Nobody Wanted:** An independent audit discovers a governance conflict or misleading product description. Player can disclose and fix, suspend a product, or face escalating regulatory and community consequences. No concealment victory path.

### Act IV — The System Pushes Back (Levels 4.16–4.20)
**4.16 The Capital Offer:** Rafiq proposes fast expansion in exchange for voting control and aggressive growth targets. Choices: accept with safeguards, decline and grow slowly, or negotiate community ownership protections.
**4.17 The Price War:** Credora improves its terms and launches a competing product. Players cannot sabotage it; compete through service, transparency and partnerships, or recognize a legitimate reform.
**4.18 Housing for Everyone:** Connect finance to an accessible housing project. Compare total cost, occupancy risk, repairs, legal tenure, member equity and a reviewed financing model.
**4.19 The Mosque's Operating Reserve:** Community leaders want a new building. Player must separate fictional donations, restricted funds, construction costs and maintenance reserve; tie into mosque-building campaign, not buy religious merit.
**4.20 Cross-Border Trade:** An ethical importer needs inventory financing. Compare currency exposure, supplier payments, insurance and legal trade compliance. Partner with the Supply Chain chapter.

### Act V — The Year Without You (Levels 4.21–4.25)
**4.21 The Downturn:** Simulate unemployment, higher costs, late payments and reduced donations; no guaranteed returns.
**4.22 The Governance Crisis:** A popular leader pressures the board to bypass risk limits. Player establishes independent oversight and a fair appeal route.
**4.23 The Rumor Run:** A misleading video causes withdrawal pressure. Use truthful, privacy-preserving communication and legitimate regulator contact, not market manipulation.
**4.24 The Successor:** Train independent management, document product rules and preserve community accountability. Test resilience without the protagonist.
**4.25 The Ledger Remains:** Ending evaluates actual fictional access, financial runway, consumer outcomes, legal compliance, transparency and institutional independence. No religious or salvation score.

## Playable systems
1. **Contract Lens:** readable fictional offer, total repayment/cost calculator, fee disclosure, consent and complaint controls. No live financial products.
2. **Financial Sandbox:** deterministic monthly cash flows, liquidity, default and reserve stress tests; currency and rates explicitly fictional.
3. **Institution Builder:** governance, staffing, licensing status, product review, risk committees, reserves, procurement and independent audit.
4. **Negotiation Engine:** dialogue with customer, worker, incumbent, scholar, regulator and investor; choices have tradeoffs rather than predetermined righteous scores.
5. **Evidence Board:** separates observed facts, contractual terms, disputed allegations, verified regulatory findings and unreviewed theological interpretations.
6. **Repair and Appeals:** error correction, restructuring, policy revision, transparency report, customer redress and safe re-entry.
7. **Real-world bridge:** optional, privacy-preserving prompts to read a real consumer disclosure, ask a qualified counselor, or learn about community finance. No real financial-data collection or compulsory offline verification.

## Fictional financial mechanics — non-authoritative
Cash runway = available unrestricted cash / projected net monthly cash outflow, when outflow is positive.
Simple loan illustration: total paid = sum of scheduled payments plus disclosed fees; never compare products solely by nominal rate.
Fund sustainability: cash inflows - disbursements - operating costs - realized losses; distinguish accounting profit from available cash.
Risk stress: simulate conservative/base/adverse scenarios with no assumption of guaranteed capital preservation.
All models are educational simulations; jurisdiction-specific accounting, consumer law and religious rulings require professional review.

## Branching endings
- **The Cooperative:** smaller, durable and accessible; slow growth, strong governance.
- **The Reformed Incumbent:** Credora adopts stronger consumer protection and collaborates; competition need not require collapse.
- **The Fragile Success:** rapid expansion helps many but reveals a liquidity gap; recovery remains possible.
- **The Transparent Reset:** player suspends an unsuitable product and rebuilds trust through independent review.
- **The Shared Infrastructure:** several institutions interoperate while remaining separately governed.

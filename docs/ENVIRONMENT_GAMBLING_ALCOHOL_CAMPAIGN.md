# Dunya — Expanded Industry Levels: Environment, Gambling Harm, Alcohol Harm
Status: **fictional mission design, not implemented gameplay, legal advice or religious instruction.** This expands the existing eleven-chapter corporate parody bible. Real industry references are categories for independent research, not allegations that named firms committed fictional acts.

## New chapter 12: The Green Mirage — VerdantWorks
**Satire targets:** greenwashing, misleading offsets, disposable products, emissions accounting, waste exports, water-intensive development. Real-world research categories: fossil fuel advertising, carbon-credit markets, fast fashion, electronics recycling and municipal waste contracting. Do not assert wrongdoing by a named real company without verified case records.

**NPCs:** Noura Aziz (environmental engineer), Grace Kim (factory worker), Tunde Adeyemi (water utility operator), Elena Park (councilor), Theo Marsh (corporate sustainability director). Each has legitimate concerns and agency.

**Level 12.1 — The River That Changed Color**
Opening: the fictional Bayhaven canal looks polluted after heavy rain. Player photographs public conditions, interviews residents and requests lawful water-quality records. **Choices:** blame a company without evidence, wait for sampling and review, or prioritize temporary community protection while evidence is gathered. The first choice requires a public correction later; no unsafe sampling or trespass gameplay.
**Level 12.2 — The Carbon Receipt**
Compare a fictional company's marketing claim with source-labelled emissions boundaries. Sort direct, energy-related and value-chain emissions in a conceptual dashboard. **Choices:** accept a glossy offset claim, commission an independent assessment, or propose a narrower verified claim.
**Level 12.3 — The Mountain of Returns**
A cheap-product warehouse generates packaging waste. Design repair, reuse, collection and sorting infrastructure. **Choices:** repair-first cooperative, deposit-return pilot, or regulated recycler partnership. All carry costs and labor safety requirements.
**Level 12.4 — Build the Watershed**
Place fictional drains, retention basins, rain gardens, pumps and safe walking routes on a graph map. Simulate storm load, capacity, maintenance and budget. No real engineering certification.
**Level 12.5 — The Year of the Flood**
A storm exposes weaknesses. Measure drainage service, public health access, accessible evacuation, electricity continuity and maintenance reserve. Repair failed systems without punishing vulnerable residents.

**Buildable institution:** Bayhaven Watershed Cooperative, a fictional water/waste/repair service with public oversight.
**Outcomes:** measured pollution indicators in simulation, water access, flood exposure, waste diversion, operating cost, worker safety and trust.
**Cross-links:** Food Chain, Supply Chain, Housing Trap, Captured City, mosque-building site planning and engineering LMS.
**Release gates:** professional environmental review, no dangerous DIY waste treatment or hazardous cleanup instructions, original assets, source-labelled claims.

## New chapter 13: The House Always Wins — LuckyOrbit
**Satire targets:** misleading odds, frictionless wagering UX, inducements, debt exposure, sports-ad saturation, affiliate incentives and consumer self-exclusion failures. No playable betting, gambling simulation for rewards, odds optimization, gambling advertisements, real-money integrations or gambling affiliate links.

**NPCs:** Aisha Rahman (consumer-rights counselor), Malik Reed (former sports promoter), Sofia Vega (community sports coach), Priya Das (regulator), Ezra Bell (fictional platform product manager). Affected characters are never mocked or blamed.

**Level 13.1 — The Match Without the Match**
A fictional sports event is surrounded by betting promotions. Player identifies advertising placement and disclosure issues from **fictional** UI cards. **Choices:** create an educational poster, request a voluntary ad-free setting, or organize a youth sports alternative. No bet is placed.
**Level 13.2 — Read the Small Print**
Inspect fictional promotional terms, expiry conditions and confusing defaults. Distinguish an observed interface from a legal conclusion. **Choices:** document concerns, ask provider for clarification, or seek a qualified consumer advocate.
**Level 13.3 — A Friend Needs Space**
An NPC discloses financial stress. Player listens without judgment, asks permission before offering support, and can direct them to a verified, jurisdiction-appropriate help resource. No disclosure is recorded as game analytics.
**Level 13.4 — The Exit That Works**
Design a fictional consumer-protection interface with accessible account controls, meaningful cooling-off options, plain-language terms and clear exit paths. This is a UX audit, not a tutorial for running a betting product.
**Level 13.5 — The Community League**
Build a non-wagering sports league and after-school recreation program with accessible facilities, staffing, safeguarding, scheduling and transparent funding. Stress-test operating costs.

**Buildable institution:** Bayhaven Community Sports and Financial Wellbeing Hub.
**Outcomes:** recreation access, voluntary support availability, advertising transparency, safeguarding, program runway.
**Cross-links:** Attention Trap, Debt Empire, Education Machine, CivicBridge.
**Release gates:** age-appropriate treatment, no real betting mechanic, qualified mental-health/addiction reviewer, privacy, regional consumer-law review.

## New chapter 14: The Night Economy — AmberGlow
**Satire targets:** aggressive alcohol marketing, impaired-driving risk, neighborhood noise, unsafe nightlife transport, pressure on workers, and conflicts between hospitality livelihoods and public health. Fictional corporation AmberGlow is not a thinly disguised allegation about a particular brewer, bar or hospitality group. No alcohol purchase, consumption, intoxication meter, alcohol reward, drinking challenge or drink-making gameplay.

**NPCs:** Mariam Bello (late-night transit planner), Daniel Cruz (hospitality worker), Ruth Chen (neighboring shopkeeper), Dr. Hawa Idris (public-health advisor), Owen Price (fictional venue manager).

**Level 14.1 — After Closing**
A pedestrian corridor has safety and access complaints after a crowded night. Player interviews workers, residents and visitors. **Choices:** document street conditions, coordinate with city transport staff, or organize a public hearing. Avoid depicting all patrons as dangerous.
**Level 14.2 — The Sponsored Festival**
Review fictional event sponsorship disclosures, youth-exposure concerns and alternatives for financing a community festival. **Choices:** independent local sponsorship, transparent restricted sponsorship review, or smaller event scale. No alcohol ads in the game.
**Level 14.3 — The Way Home**
Design late-night bus stops, lighting, step-free routes and safe ride options in the fictional city map. Compare cost, staffing, accessibility and service coverage. No driving while impaired or pursuit gameplay.
**Level 14.4 — The Worker Behind the Counter**
An NPC wants safer working conditions and alternative career pathways. Player can support training, labor consultation and lawful transition to a non-alcohol hospitality business, without shaming employees.
**Level 14.5 — A Different Kind of Night**
Build a family-friendly evening economy: tea houses, arts, late-night food, community sports and transport. Model business margins, noise, accessibility, staff wages and community feedback.

**Buildable institution:** Bayhaven Evening Cooperative, a fictional alcohol-free hospitality and mobility network.
**Outcomes:** safe travel access, worker income, neighborhood noise, business viability, youth protection and community participation.
**Cross-links:** Healthcare Monopoly, Housing Trap, Captured City, Supply Chain, Food Chain.
**Release gates:** public-health reviewer, local licensing-law reviewer, no stigmatizing portrayals, no practical impaired-driving or alcohol sales mechanics.

## Shared systems: what makes these deeper than fetch quests
- **Evidence board:** observation, testimony, contractual term, regulator finding and unverified allegation are distinct.
- **Resource graphs:** water, electricity, streets, staffing, budget, waste, supply chains and accessible paths must balance.
- **Corporation negotiation:** the fictional rival may reform, compete, partner or reject proposals. No automatic evil outcome.
- **Community consultation:** different residents and workers have competing priorities; each has meaningful agency.
- **Operations:** maintenance, labor, permits, cash runway, safety and external audits continue after a mission.
- **Repair paths:** erroneous public claims can be corrected; failing infrastructure can be redesigned; programs can scale down without moral failure.
- **Real-world action:** optional cards invite reading reputable resources, attending public meetings or joining vetted community initiatives. No tracking of prayer, addiction, abstinence or personal health status.

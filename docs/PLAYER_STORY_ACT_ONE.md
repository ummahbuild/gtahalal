# Dunya — Act I: The Neighborhood We Share
Status: original fictional player-facing narrative; **not religious instruction**. Intended for the starter neighborhood. This is a content design deliverable; only the earlier pantry interaction is partly implemented.

## Logline
A new resident arrives in Bayhaven, a fictional coastal neighborhood where an overstretched community pantry, a misleading viral clip and a struggling local business collide. Ahmed or Fatimah can help neighbors uncover the real problems, repair trust and create a durable community service without blaming or humiliating anyone.

## Cast and motivations
- **Sara Rahman**, pantry volunteer coordinator: compassionate and exhausted; wants the pantry to remain accessible, fears losing volunteers.
- **Amina Yusuf**, pantry inventory lead: careful with resources; wants accurate stock records and predictable delivery schedules.
- **Bilal Carter**, bicycle mechanic: notices unsafe curb access; wants a lawful, accessible loading zone.
- **Maya Ellis**, local journalist: wants the full story rather than a viral clip; willing to publish corrections.
- **Omar Haddad**, small grocer: worries that free food distribution will harm his shop; may become a cooperative supplier if treated fairly.

These characters are original fictional composites; names are not representations of actual people.

## Episode 0 — A Place to Begin
Opening camera: warm morning on Harbor Street, a bus stop, shops and a community center visible. No borrowed cinematic shots.
Sara: "You look new around here. I'm Sara. If you have a moment, there's something we could use help with."
Choices:
A. "What does the neighborhood need?" -> Sara explains pantry demand.
B. "I'm still finding my way around." -> Sara points out the plaza and transit stop; mission remains available.
C. "Not right now." -> respectful exit; no penalty.
Objective: Find Sara and learn where the pantry is. Completion: talk or choose to return later.

## Episode 1 — The Pantry Route
Sara: "Amina has supplies waiting, but our delivery route is confusing. Could you check in with her?"
Amina: "Thanks for coming. Before we move anything, we need a clear count and a safe route."
Choices:
A. Help organize the inventory -> learn item count and destination.
B. Ask about accessibility -> identify a blocked curb and alternate route.
C. Decline today -> no penalty, can return.
Gameplay: travel from Sara to Amina, check supply ledger, choose accessible route. Completion: confirmed handoff, not simply tapping NPC.
Repair: mistaken count can be corrected before distribution.
Prototype mapping: current implemented quest is a simplified Sara-to-Amina interaction; inventory inspection and route choice remain future work.

## Episode 2 — The Cropped Video
A short clip appears to show pantry volunteers turning someone away.
Maya: "This is spreading fast. But what happened before the camera started?"
Omar: "People are blaming the pantry and my store. Neither story is complete."
Choices:
A. Share the clip -> consequence: local trust declines; player can issue correction.
B. Speak to affected visitors, request consent and verify records -> discover the actual problem.
C. Avoid online debate and help resolve access in person -> equally valid service path.
Twist: the clip is misleading, but an inaccessible entrance genuinely needs fixing.
Gameplay: evidence journal; separate verified facts from interpretations; never publish private beneficiary details.
Review leads: Qur'an 49:6 as a source candidate, not yet approved game instruction.

## Episode 3 — The Curb and the Contract
Bilal: "That delivery van blocks the ramp every afternoon. We can fix the route without shutting anyone out."
Omar: "I can supply staples at cost if we agree on clear payment terms."
Choices:
A. Request a permitted loading arrangement from the fictional city.
B. Use bicycle deliveries with accessibility checks.
C. Negotiate a transparent supplier agreement and schedule.
Tradeoffs: cost, volunteer hours, pedestrian safety, supply reliability. No automatic perfect outcome.
Gameplay: map-based route, cost comparison and community consultation.

## Episode 4 — Build Something That Lasts
Sara: "One good day helped. What happens next week when we're all busy?"
Choices:
A. Establish a volunteer rota and handoff notes.
B. Partner with Omar's shop and track fair procurement.
C. Create a transparent community update board with privacy protections.
Ending variants: accessible pantry, resilient supplier relationship, better information flow. Each can be improved later.
Persistent outcomes: volunteer availability, supply reliability, neighborhood trust, accessibility improvements.
